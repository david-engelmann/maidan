### The landing is a journey: a night square, a lift through liquid light, and the square again

- **Changed:** the landing's picture is now one scene behind the whole page, driven by the scroll, in place of the gathering. The hero is a night square in the spirit of Madison Square Park, seen from a low camera: a fountain of liquid light on the axis (a basin, a jet, eight streams arcing into a glowing pool, rills in the paving, a surge every ten seconds), a flat-iron wedge of a building lit behind it, a clock tower, arcades, street trees and lamps, wet paving that mirrors the fountain and the lamps, and people as small figures carrying lights. On a wide screen the fountain stands right of centre and the words have a quiet left third; on a phone the fountain crowns the first screen over the headline and the first call to action.
- **Changed:** scrolling lifts the camera through the fountain's plume, molten folds that close round the frame, into the same liquid at rest: indigo and umber silk with thin ember veins behind the sections. A ribbon of light crosses the gap over "How it works" and over "Hosted or self-hosted", and the scroll and the cursor stir the folds. At the closing call to action the liquid drains back to the fountain and the camera pulls back, so the square's eight rills read as rays. Behind the header and every block of copy the picture is stopped down and held under the luminance that keeps body text at 4.5:1 or better against the worst pixel, measured down the whole page at 1440 and 390 pixels wide in both themes.
- **Changed:** on paper the square is travertine in a low sun with long shadows, the fountain is water carrying terracotta ink, and the liquid behind the sections is cream marble with terracotta veins.
- **Changed:** the scene is raw WebGL2 with no dependency, in its own chunk (`/landing/journey.js`, about 27 KB gzipped), loaded once the page has loaded and gone idle. Until it draws, a CSS poster of the square (an SVG in the stylesheet, one per theme) holds the hero, and it stays wherever WebGL2 is missing, fails, or is drawn on the CPU, so the first paint, its largest element and the layout do not move when the scene arrives. The city is ray-marched only when the camera moves, at a fraction of the frame the GPU keeps up with, and a band a frame at full size once it rests, with a cross-fade between the two. The scene measures its own frame time and steps its pixel ratio (capped at 2), simulation size and march length down when it misses frames. It stops in a hidden tab, and one pause control travels with the screen. Under reduced motion it is a composed still per act, redrawn as the page scrolls.
- **Changed:** the sections stand further apart where the journey's beats fall (under the hero, over "How it works" and over "Hosted or self-hosted"), and where the scene is drawing the closing section takes the whole screen.
- **Removed:** the gathering and its chunk (`/landing/gathering.js`) from the landing, and the separate pause controls in the hero and the close.
